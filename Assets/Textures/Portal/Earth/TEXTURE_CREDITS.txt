Orbital Earth texture sources

Cloud-free surface:
NASA Blue Marble: Next Generation, July 2004, global base map.
https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/blue-marble-next-generation/base-map/
Source: world.200407.3x21600x10800.jpg (21600 x 10800).
Prepared EarthSurface_NASA_8K.jpg: resized to 8192 x 4096, JPEG quality 95.
Credit: NASA Earth Observatory, Reto Stockli / Robert Simmon and the MODIS team.

Night city lights:
NASA Black Marble 2016, grayscale global map, 3 km.
https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/earth-at-night/maps/
Source: BlackMarble_2016_3km_gray.jpg (13500 x 6750).
Prepared EarthNight_NASA_4K.png: resized to 4096 x 2048, single-channel PNG.
Credit: NASA Earth Observatory / Joshua Stevens; Suomi NPP VIIRS data from Miguel Roman, NASA GSFC.
The underlying datasets are historical composites, not live weather or current city activity.
NASA imagery usage information: https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/

Clouds:
Solar System Scope / INOVE, Earth Clouds, 8K equirectangular map.
https://www.solarsystemscope.com/textures/
https://www.solarsystemscope.com/textures/download/8k_earth_clouds.jpg
License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International.
https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/
Prepared EarthClouds_SSS_8K.png: converted to a grayscale optical-density mask and retained
at its original 8192 x 4096 resolution. Cloud movement, shading and shadows are simulated.
Include this attribution when redistributing the application/assets.

All maps use matching equirectangular longitude/latitude coordinates. No generative images
or randomly placed cities are used. Existing imported Earth sphere is subdivided once for a smoother orbital horizon.
